Overview

A machine learning system that classifies music tracks into one of eight genres by analysing the physics of sound. Rather than treating audio as a black box, we extract meaningful mathematical descriptors — frequency, timbre, energy, rhythm — using Librosa and train classic classifiers with Scikit-learn.

03
Feature Extraction

Each 30-second clip is loaded as a mono waveform at 22,050 Hz. Eight acoustic descriptor families are computed using Librosa. Time-varying features are summarised by their mean and standard deviation, producing a fixed-length vector of 75 numerical features per track.

MFCC 20 coefficients × 2 stats = 40
Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients. Captures timbral texture and vocal character by mimicking human auditory perception on a mel frequency scale. Distinguishes electric guitar (Rock) from synthesiser bass (Electronic).
Chroma STFT 12 pitch classes × 2 stats = 24
Projects audio energy onto the 12 semitone pitch classes (C, C#, D … B). Captures harmonic content and chord progressions independent of octave.
Spectral Centroid mean + std = 2
The centre of mass of the frequency spectrum — a measure of brightness. High centroid = bright/sharp (cymbals, brass). Low centroid = warm/deep (bass, cello).
Spectral Bandwidth mean + std = 2
The spread of frequencies around the centroid. Wide bandwidth indicates a rich, full sound; narrow bandwidth suggests a pure tone.
Spectral Rolloff mean + std = 2
The frequency below which 85% of total spectral energy lies. Separates high-energy treble-dominant genres from bass-heavy or muffled recordings.
Zero-Crossing Rate mean + std = 2
How rapidly the waveform crosses the zero-amplitude axis. High ZCR indicates percussive or noisy content; low ZCR indicates sustained harmonic sounds.
RMS Energy mean + std = 2
Root-mean-square amplitude — a direct measure of perceived loudness and dynamic power range across the clip.
Tempo 1 scalar
Estimated rhythmic speed in beats per minute (BPM), computed via dynamic programming beat tracking. Unmeasured signals fall back to the dataset median of 118 BPM.

PREPROCESSING NOTE
Short live microphone recordings (<15 s) are seamlessly tiled to 30 s before feature extraction so the window statistics match the training distribution. All signals are RMS-normalised to a target amplitude of 0.18 before feature computation.

1
Load & Split
Features CSV is loaded. Non-feature columns (track_id, genre) are dropped. Stratified 80/20 train-test split is applied — preserving class balance in both sets.
2
Feature Scaling
StandardScaler is fit exclusively on the training set, then applied to both sets. This prevents data leakage from test statistics into the model.
3
5-Fold Cross-Validation
Stratified K-Fold CV (k=5) is run on the training set for each model. Macro F1 is the primary scoring metric, giving equal weight to every genre regardless of frequency.
4
Test Evaluation
Each model is retrained on the full training set and evaluated once on the held-out test set. Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and Macro F1 are recorded.
5
Model Selection & Persistence
The model with highest Test Macro F1 is selected. The model, scaler, and feature list are serialised with joblib for inference.
6
Inference (Ensemble)
At prediction time, SVM probability outputs and Random Forest probability outputs are averaged. The class with the highest averaged probability is the final prediction.

Best Model — SVM with RBF Kernel

Support Vector Machine with a Radial Basis Function kernel achieved the highest performance across all metrics. At inference time it is combined with Random Forest in a soft-voting ensemble, averaging predicted class probabilities to improve robustness on unseen audio.

WHY SVM WINS
SVM excels in high-dimensional feature spaces (75 features, 6,400 samples). The RBF kernel finds non-linear decision boundaries that separate genres whose acoustic features partially overlap (e.g. Experimental vs. Electronic). Because features are StandardScaler-normalised first, SVM's distance-based margin is not distorted by scale differences between MFCC coefficients and Tempo.
